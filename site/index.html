Project for Vermeer · GPS-free drone navigation

How fast does camera-only navigation drift?

I built a monocular visual odometry pipeline, ran it on real drone flights with motion-capture ground truth, and stress-tested it under noise, blur, darkness, dropped frames and flat terrain, to measure exactly how a camera's position estimate drifts away from the truth.

0.61 mposition error (ATE) over an 80 m drone flight
6.5%drift per 10 m flown, roughly constant with distance
13controlled stress experiments on the same flight
~15×more drift over flat ground than ground with depth

02 · Method

What I built

  1. 1

    Track

    Shi-Tomasi corners tracked frame to frame with pyramidal KLT optical flow; new keyframe after 10 px of image motion.

  2. 2

    Estimate motion

    Essential matrix with RANSAC, then rotation and direction of travel. Steps with few inliers or implausible rotation are rejected.

  3. 3

    Chain poses

    Relative motions chained into a trajectory, converted from the camera frame to the drone body frame (IMU) for comparison.

  4. 4

    Evaluate

    ATE after SE3 alignment and relative error (RPE) over 2, 5, 10 and 20 m segments with evo; one script reruns everything.

Scope, stated plainly: a single camera cannot measure absolute scale, so step length comes from ground truth (“oracle scale”). The drift measured here is rotation and direction drift, the part a camera is responsible for.

04 · The drone-specific risk

Flat ground breaks a downward camera

A synthetic downward-looking flight (24 m arc at 5 m height, exact ground truth) over the same texture, once with raised 1 m blocks and once perfectly flat.

Over a perfectly flat scene the essential matrix has two valid solutions, and the pipeline picks the wrong one often enough to multiply drift. Fields, desert, water and car parks look like this to a drone. A production system needs plane-aware (homography) estimation or an IMU to break the ambiguity.

05 · Findings

What the data says

Drift grows linearly with distance

6.5–8% at every segment length from 2 to 20 m. VO alone can't hold position over long flights; it needs a correction source such as an IMU, map matching or satellite imagery.

Flat terrain is the worst case

About 3% drift per 10 m with depth in the scene, about 55% over flat ground. Repeatable across runs.

Image noise hurts the most

Short-range drift rose from 8% to 12% per 2 m and ATE more than doubled at σ = 30 (0.61 → 1.44 m): noise shifts the corners that KLT tracks.

Blur and dropped frames cost robustness first

Drift per 10 m barely moved, but lost or rejected steps rose from 2 to 27 (heavy blur) and 36 (every 4th frame): the system gets close to losing track before accuracy changes.

Darkness alone did little

Tracking ignores uniform brightness changes. Real low light adds sensor noise and motion blur, and the noise is what does the damage.

One run per condition: differences under about 2 percentage points are within run-to-run variation, so read the trends, not single cells.

06 · Debugging

Two bugs worth knowing about

  • Camera vs body frame. EuRoC ground truth is the IMU body's pose. Comparing camera poses to it directly made relative errors look 7× worse; converting with the camera-to-body transform fixed it.
  • OpenCV's depth cutoff. recoverPose drops points farther than 50 step lengths by default. In a large hall with small steps that threw away most points and rejected two thirds of valid motion estimates.

07 · Next

Where I'd take it

  • Fuse the IMU gyro for rotation, removing most heading drift.
  • Plane-aware (homography) motion estimation for downward cameras over flat terrain.
  • Bound drift by matching against maps or satellite imagery.
  • Benchmark against OpenVINS and ORB-SLAM3 on the same flights.
